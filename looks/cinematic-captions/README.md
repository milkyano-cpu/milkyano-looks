# Cinematic Captions

Captions on an untouched talking-head clip: a quiet verbatim rail carries the words, and at the peak one hero word is composited behind the subject so the person passes in front of it.

## What you get

- Output: the source talking-head clip, footage untouched, with captions added in two layers: a front rail (subtitle) and a back "embed" layer that the subject's matte occludes.
- Aspect: follows the source (9:16, 16:9 or 1:1). Positions below are given for 1080x1920.
- fps: follows the source (the matte is extracted at the source's native rate).
- Length: any single-shot clip over 3 s. Multi-shot footage is split and captioned shot by shot.
- Delivered: `final.mp4` (rail + embed modes) or `final_fx.mp4` (themed identities, after the plate reaction), plus preview frames and a contact sheet.

## The look in detail

### Type
- Default identity (`anchor`): Sora for body and hero (free Google font). Other identities bring their own free fonts (Inter, Bodoni Moda, Anton, Press Start 2P and so on); see the catalog in provenance.
- Rail: one clean sans, weight 500-600, near-white. Size `0.045 x frame height` (about 86 px on a 1920-tall frame; the `anchor` theme ships `fontPx` 46 with letter-spacing 0.012em). At most 2 lines, 32-42 characters per line, broken at a clause, never a dangling one-word line.
- Hero (embed climax): sized to the frame, not the rail: `0.16-0.22 x frame height` (about 307-422 px on 1920 tall; default ~0.18-0.20). Uppercase 800-900 for punch identities. The `anchor` hero ships at 150 px. Hierarchy floor: hero at least 1.8x body emphasis and 2.5x the smallest tier.
- Embed body tiers (column-flow identities): about 0.055h italic narration, 0.085-0.10h emphasis, 0.20h crown.
- Never animate letter-spacing or blur on word entrances (inline reflow makes lines jump).

### Colour roles
| role | default (`anchor`) | used for |
|---|---|---|
| rail_text | `#F2EFE9` | rail body text |
| accent | `#D9B98C` | hero underline, drawn accent line |
| emphasis | `#EFCB9A` | inline emphasis words on the rail |
| hero_text | `#FFF5DF` | warm cream hero for screen-blend identities |
| scrim | `rgba(0,0,0,0.35)` | soft text-box scrim, 30-40 %, sized to the text, never full-frame |

Column-flow identities sample their accent from the scene (fallback `#E3C06A` for `cream`). Luminance decides the scheme: caption region luma under 60 = light text as-is; 60-180 = add a glyph scrim; over 180 = opaque text plus scrim, and screen-blend looks are out (use `ink` or a rail surface).

### Motion
- Rail: 150-250 ms fade-up in, fade-down out (`anchor`: 2-frame fade-ups at 30 fps). Inline emphasis may pop to at most 1.1x. No glitch, no per-word choreography on the rail.
- Default word move (column flow): per word, 80 ms stagger, opacity 0 to 1 and y 8 to 0 px, 0.45 s `power2.out`.
- `cream` DNA presets: soft (y 8, blur 2.5, 0.5 s `power2.out`), present (y 6, scale 1.03, 0.3 s `power3.out`), impact (y 12, scale 1.08, 0.24 s `power4.out`), exit (y -8, 0.45 s `sine.in`).
- Hero three-act, generated by the engine: co-visible captions dim (to 0.45-0.55) as setup, per-letter entrance (16 ms letter stagger in `cream`) with amplitude tied to the spoken loudness, then breathe (1.00 to 1.008-1.012 over ~2 s, sine) and glow until the exit.
- `anchor` hero: the `settle` setpiece, the word settles into the scene behind the subject with a drawn accent underline. The rail yields while it plays: dims to 0.55 from 0.2 s before to 0.9 s after.
- Plate reaction (themed identities only, applied after the matte composite so subject, text and plate move as one): `anchor` dims the plate 0.16, pushes in 1.2 %, grain 5.
- Exit is the entrance reversed at 60 % duration. Never fade out during speech, only in the gap.
- Deterministic: no `Math.random()`, no `Date.now()`, no infinite repeats.

### Captions
Every spoken phrase is one of three things:

| class | what | shown as |
|---|---|---|
| drop | filler, stutters, self-corrections | not shown |
| rail | ordinary content, verbatim | lower-third subtitle in front, one group at a time |
| embed | the promoted peak word | big hero behind the subject, designed entrance and exit |

- The rail carries most of the text. Embed is scarce: at most 1 hero per thought, never two on screen together, at least ~0.6 s of air between hero windows, one apex per clip (the largest hero; the rest are minor peaks).
- Rail position on 9:16: lower-middle, 600-700 px from the bottom (baseline about y 1220-1320 on 1920), clear of platform UI, inside the 80 % title-safe box. The `anchor` theme ships `bottomPx` 96; raise it on vertical deliveries.
- The hero sits on the subject, 30-55 % occluded by the person. Face never 100 % covered: in every 0.3 s window at least 30 % of the face box stays clear.
- Each caption at least 0.5 s on screen. Word timings within 80 ms of the transcript. One transcript word per timed entry.
- Reading order: on-screen top-to-bottom order matches spoken order.

### Graphics and elements
- The footage is never graded, recoloured, scanlined or vignetted. Texture lives inside caption elements only. Themed identities are the one exception, through their plate reaction.
- The matte is the person (human segmentation). Props near the subject can leak into it; held objects can drop out. Check two or three matte frames before placing the hero.
- 35 identities: 10 classic visual languages (cream, ink, editorial, keynote, documentary, loud, neon, glitch, chrome, velocity) and 25 themed worlds, each a rail or panel paradigm plus a hero setpiece plus front effects plus plate reaction (anchor, ordnance, terminal, neonsign, stardust, stomp, lastpage, scoreboard, transit, vhs, arcade, dossier, laser, thunder, hologram, biolume, aurora, spectrum, papercut, popup, chalkboard, graffiti, brush, inkwater, ransom).

### Choosing an identity
- Every word must be read (explainer, tutorial, news, accessibility): a rail or panel surface. `anchor`, `ordnance`, `terminal`.
- "Embed it / cinematic / premium": a column-flow identity by register and scene brightness. Bright scene: `ink`. Warm dark scene: `cream`. Fashion: `editorial`. Product launch: `keynote`. Serious interview: `documentary`. Hype or sport: `loud` or `velocity`.
- VFX asks ("make it explode", "like After Effects"): pick by what should explode. A word: `ordnance`. The whole frame: `stomp`. A decode: `terminal`. Every beat: `loud`.
- Scene vetoes last: luma over 180 kills screen-blend column looks; dusk or dark unlocks `neon` / `neonsign`.
- Unsure: `anchor`. Shortlist 2-3, recommend one with a one-line reason, the editor picks.

### Sound
None added. The clip's own audio passes through untouched. Hero amplitude follows the speech loudness envelope.

### Pacing and cut rhythm
- No cuts: single shot, untouched timing. The rail follows speech phrase by phrase with about 1.5 s minimum gap discipline so it does not strobe.
- A short clip gets 1-2 heroes; a long explainer about one per section.

## Do / Don't

Do
- Probe first: shot cuts at 20 / 50 / 80 %, letterbox bars, caption-region luminance, a 1 fps contact sheet for burned-in text.
- Preview composite frames before paying for a render.
- Keep the hero big and visibly behind the subject.

Don't
- Don't embed every word. That is the common mistake.
- Don't caption clips with several speakers, hard cuts, no face, no speech, garbage transcripts or burned-in captions.
- Don't use light screen-blend text on bright scenes.
- Don't let two caption groups overlap in time and screen space.
- Don't grade the footage.

## How to make it

1. Decision gate: `ffprobe` the clip, sample frames at 20 / 50 / 80 %, make a 1 fps contact sheet. Refuse or split as above.
2. Shortlist 2-3 identities, recommend one, the editor picks.
3. Set up a HyperFrames project with the clip, then run the embedded-captions prepare step: subject matte, word-level transcript (WhisperX), audio envelope and safe zones (in parallel).
4. Author the one small JSON for the identity: `cinematic.json` (blocks of 2-5 word lines, at most one hero line per block) for column-flow identities, or `theme.json` (verbatim lines, minors, `hero.match`) for themed ones. Read the safe zones first; place narration in the strips hugging the silhouette.
5. Preview composite frames (~2 s each). Check washout, text-on-text, reading order, hero presence, balance. Fix the JSON and re-preview.
6. Render and composite. The gates check timing, occlusion and hero, overflow and hand-off.
7. Deliver `final.mp4` / `final_fx.mp4` with `editor-grade-motion` QA.

Needs the `embedded-captions` skill (third-party, see provenance), Node with sharp, puppeteer and gsap in the project, ffmpeg, bash, and `uvx` for WhisperX.

## Skin slots

| slot | controls |
|---|---|
| `identity` | which of the 35 identities to use |
| `font_body` | rail / body font (free fonts only) |
| `font_hero` | hero word font |
| `rail_text` | rail text colour |
| `accent` | hero accent and underline colour |
| `emphasis` | inline emphasis word colour on the rail |
| `scrim` | text-box scrim colour and alpha |
| `rail_bottom_px` | rail distance from the bottom edge |
| `hero_case` | hero case (upper, lower, as spoken) |
| `language` | transcript language for WhisperX |
